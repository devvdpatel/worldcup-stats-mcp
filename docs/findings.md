## Headline findings
1. **No clear World Cup effect.** For every KPI the 95% interval for the difference in per-90 change includes
   zero (smallest p = 0.06, assists). Directionally,
   World Cup players' pooled xG/90 rose 0.129 → 0.157 while others
   were flat (0.132 → 0.133), and their assists/90 fell
   (0.120 → 0.084).
2. **Rest and tournament load are not associated with the change.** World Cup players returned a median
   19 days after their nation's last match (range 12–90). Spearman ρ between
   rest days and g+/90 change = +0.02 (p = 0.93); between World Cup minutes
   and g+/90 change = -0.23 (p = 0.26); n = 26.
3. **Clubs: players sent is not associated with the change in xG difference.** Across 30 clubs, ρ(World Cup
   players sent, change in xGD/game) = +0.05 (p = 0.81). The 21
   clubs that sent at least one player changed by +0.33 xGD/game more than the
   9 that sent none (p = 0.19).
4. **The biggest load fell on Inter Miami CF**: 1,231 World Cup minutes from
   2 players; its xGD/game went +0.69 →
   +0.14. Los Angeles FC sent the most players (4).
   Single clubs are anecdotes, not evidence.
5. **Lionel Messi** (Inter Miami, 740 World Cup minutes, 8 goals, Argentina runner-up) was back in MLS 14 days after the final. His xG/90 went 0.71 → 0.76 (1,420 → 1,629 minutes) and stayed at the 100th / 100th percentile for xG/90 among wingers with >= 450 minutes (74 pre / 87 post).

Also: World Cup players were already heavier-used regulars before the break (median
93 vs 85 minutes per appearance), which is
why raw comparisons between the two groups are confounded.
